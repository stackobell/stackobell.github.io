WHAT CHANGED IN THIS PASS
=========================

VOICE: FIRST PERSON SINGULAR
- All declarative "we" is now "I". Two instances changed on marketing.html.
- "We'll look at your campaigns" in the CTAs is left alone on purpose. There
  "we" means you and the client, which is correct and reads as collaborative.
- The FAQ questions written in the client's voice ("which one should we run?")
  are also left alone. That's them talking, not you.
- Added the network statement in two places: marketing.html FAQ 04, and a full
  paragraph on about.html. It gives you the team capability without the agency
  claim.

NEW: about.html
- Added to the main nav and the footer on every page.
- Sections: who you are, where the experience comes from, a credentials list,
  how you work, and what you don't do.
- The "What I don't do" section is deliberate. Naming what you turn down is
  the strongest credibility signal on a consultant's site and it kills bad-fit
  enquiries before they cost you a call.

BRANDS SECTION: SPLIT INTO TWO HONEST GROUPS
- "Consultancy clients": Renault Ireland (video production), DCU (web design).
- "Brands I've marketed in-house": McGuirks Golf, Bewley's, Windsor Motors,
  Nissan Ireland.
- Each logo now carries a caption line. Three need filling in.
- This matters. A single undifferentiated logo wall implied all six were
  client engagements. Splitting them is more honest and, because the two real
  consultancy clients now stand on their own, it actually reads stronger.
- Note: displaying former employers' logos is normal on a portfolio, but the
  labelling has to be unambiguous, which is what this split does. If any
  employer contract has a restriction on using their marks, check it.

HOMEPAGE HERO: REWRITTEN
- The h1 was the brand name, which said nothing and burned the largest line on
  the page. The wordmark is now a styled div (still the visual anchor), and the
  h1 is a real proposition: "Senior paid media and e-commerce expertise,
  without the agency retainer."
- Intro rewritten: names you, says who you work with, drops "frameworks".
- Set in sentence case rather than the site's usual caps. Three lines of
  all-caps is hard to read, and this is the one line that must be read.
- Old copy if you want it back:
  "Building high-performance digital marketing frameworks that scale your
   business online and offline. If you're an ambitious SME looking for a
   no-waffle approach to paid ads, conversion audits, and commercial
   reporting, you're in the right place."

CTAS: STILL ONE PER PAGE, BUT SPECIFIC
- index / about:  Book a free consultation
- marketing:      Get an ad spend review
- ecommerce:      Request a conversion audit
- strategy:       Book a data review
- Every CTA now has a secondary plain-text email link under it, for people who
  won't fill in a form.
